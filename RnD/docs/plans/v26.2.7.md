## Diagnosis (Opus, 2026-10-31)

- **The gap is a lens error, not a depth error.** On building bases that stand on a surface, depth / *pinhole* ground distance is a
  median of 1.24–1.80, but depth / *lens* ground ray (`groundfuse.ground_z`, the equidistant fisheye with the horizon pitch) is
  **0.86–1.14** (0 s 1.07, 3 s 1.05, 10 s 0.99, 60 s 0.93, 120 s 1.14, 200 s 0.86). Depth puts the building where it is. The map's
  surface projection (`Observer._ground_surfaces`) uses the pinhole formulas of the collision pass (`ground_distance_m`, `lateral_m`
  inverted) on a 118° fisheye, so the sidewalk is drawn 20–80 % too near and stops short of the building: the unknown cells between.
  Snapping the building onto the pinhole sidewalk would make every wall wrong; the ground has to go through the lens.
- **Lateral, also pinhole.** `lateral_m` = (x − 0.5) W d / f_pin. On the 118° fisheye an image column a quarter of the width off centre
  is 29.5° off axis; pinhole puts it at 39.7°, ~45 % too wide (exact at the centre and at the edge). It is used for the items'
  `lateralM` (map markers, the map's corridor test |right| ≤ `laneHalfWidthM`, inside-solid snapping) and for the map's object bases.
  The **spoken clock is already lens-correct** (`bearing_deg` takes the projection). Ground-source item distances
  (`ground_distance_m`) are pinhole too; the ground modes' `groundfuse.ground_m` is already lens-correct.
- **Sidewalk through the building:** nothing stops surface votes from cells behind a building face (frame-to-frame face placement and
  pose shifts, the street next to a facade's end). The map treats a building as a 0.3 m line with the street going on behind it.
- **Ray scan of the stored map** (rays from the user every 0.5°, ±80°; *gap* = a ray whose first face cell is preceded by unknown
  cells that follow a surface cell; *through* = a ray with surface cells within 15 m after leaving the first face run):

  | t (s) | 0 | 1 | 3 | 10 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 105 | 120 | 150 | 200 | 250 | 300 | 350 | 400 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | gap rays | 91 | 61 | 118 | 13 | 15 | 9 | 34 | 38 | 4 | 0 | 12 | 0 | 8 | 0 | 0 |
  | through rays | 17 | 64 | 84 | 108 | 92 | 87 | 154 | 124 | 245 | 176 | 66 | 98 | 39 | 44 | 321 |

  The user's 95:109 / 66:89 (cell col / row) are building in 251 / 66 frames, with a surface layer under them in 115 / 41.
- **Depth correction reach:** the range-correction curve (`groundfuse.Corrector`) is fitted on walking-region pixels only, so it rarely
  reaches past ~12.5 m (the squash starts there). Wall bases standing on the ground give (depth, lens ground ray) pairs at the wall's
  range: a free calibration where it is most needed (the user's headsup).
- **Wall line:** the yellow line in the Lab is the face / edge test's fitted RANSAC line (`faceEdgeLines`, drawn from the user's row to
  8 m): a straight fit of wall cells 0–8 m ahead, not the building's edge.
- **No coordinates:** `cellText()` returns the bare word `unmapped` for a cell with no surface, no object and no item.

## Changes

New parameters go in `common/config/worldmap.json` (version → `v26.2.7`) or `collision.json` `distance.groundDepth` (version bumped),
all documented in CONTRACTS (§7.3b, §8.1). Rectilinear runs must come out **identical** to the pinhole formulas (they are exact there).

1. **One lens ground geometry** (`src/groundfuse.py`, next to `ground_z`; pure numpy, vectorised):
   - `ray_dir(geo, row, col)` → (xn, yn) normalised ray of a grid position (the code already inside `ground_z`);
   - `ground_point(geo, row, col, horizon)` → (forward m, right m, camera axis, no body offset) of the floor point seen there:
     z = `ground_z`, forward = z (cos φ − yn sin φ), right = xn z (φ = the horizon pitch, as in `ground_z`); nan above the horizon;
   - `ground_pixel(geo, fwd, right, horizon)` → (row, col) float grid position that sees floor point (fwd, right): the exact inverse
     (camera ray of the point with the pitch, then equidistant or pinhole projection). Unit test: round trip < 0.01 px / 1 mm on a grid
     of pixels, both projections;
   - `lateral_at(geo, row, col, fwd)` → right (m) of the pixel's ray at forward distance `fwd`: xn · fwd / (cos φ − yn sin φ).
   Camera height × the frame's gait scale goes in `geo.cam_h` as now (a `Geometry` copy or a `cam_h` argument; no global state).
2. **The map through the lens** (`src/worldmap.py`; `lateralModel` gets the new default `"geometry"`; `"collision"` / `"lens"` stay as A/B):
   - `_ground_surfaces`: map cell (fwd, right − lateralOffsetM) → `ground_pixel` → class of that pixel (replaces the pinhole inverse);
   - `object_bases`: the ground distance of a contact base = `ground_point` forward; the lateral of every base = `lateral_at` at its
     distance (+ lateralOffsetM);
   - `objectClearBottomM` band: the forward distance of the last image row on the centre column through `ground_point`.
3. **Items' lateral and ground distance through the lens** (`src/collision.py`): every `lateral_m(...)` call → `lateral_at` with the
   item's base row / column and distance; every `ground_distance_m(...)` → `ground_point` forward (with the gait-scaled camera height
   where the call uses it). `bearing_deg` / clock are unchanged. The `lateral_m` / `ground_distance_m` functions stay (tests, other
   callers) but the pass no longer calls them on a fisheye run.
4. **Walls calibrate the depth** (user's headsup; `groundfuse.Corrector`, `collision.json` `distance.groundDepth`):
   - new helper `worldmap.face_contact_samples(observer, grid, dmap, depth_scale, horizon)` → (depth m, lens ground z) of the face-class
     bases that stand on a surface (same base / contact / bottom-6-rows-median rules as `object_bases`, no dcorr involved);
   - the collision pass calls it on each frame before `corrector.update` and passes the pairs as `extra=(v, g)`; they join **the range
     bins only** (not `ref` q, which stays the walking region's near field), each base counting `wallSampleWeight` (5) towards
     `minBinSamples`; range up to `fuseMaxM`. The report adds `"wallSamples": n` (omitted when 0);
   - expected effect: the curve reaches the walls' range (12–20 m) instead of stopping at ~12.5 m. It changes ground-contact /
     ground+depth distances, not the default `depth` mode's.
5. **Building edge at the sidewalk limit** (`object_bases`, `faceGroundContact: true`; now with the lens ground, depth and ground agree to
   ~10 %, so this closes the remaining seam): a face-class base standing on a surface takes the lens ground-contact distance of its base row
   (`ground_point`, the same geometry as the surface projection, so the sidewalk and the face meet in one cell). **Occluded faces follow**
   (`faceScaleOccluded: true`): per frame and per face class, ratio = median(ground / depth) over its contact bases (≥ `faceRatioMinBases`
   10, clamped to [0.6, 1.5]); the class's other bases (standing on a car, pole, sign…) take depth × ratio, else keep depth. Done before
   `_smooth_faces`. Non-face objects unchanged.
6. **Buildings block the view** (`occluderClasses: ["building"]`, `surfaceHiddenFactor: 0.3`): in `observe`, for every occluder face point,
   the cells on its ray from (face distance + `faceThicknessM`) to `maxRangeM` are hidden (`self.ray_hidden`, vectorised like
   `_face_rays`), minus the cells this frame itself sees as surface (visible ground wins). In `Fusion.update`, world cells that map to
   hidden ego cells multiply their **surface** votes by `surfaceHiddenFactor` (object votes untouched; mirror `_clear_stale_objects`).
7. **Gap closing in the snapshot** (`gapCloseM: 0.5`, in `Fusion.snapshot`, so the collision pass and the stored map agree): an unknown cell
   of the combined map whose ray toward the user meets a surface cell within `gapCloseM` and whose ray away from the user meets a
   face-class cell within `gapCloseM` takes that face class (and its conf); also in the `object` layer. Ray-neighbour index tables
   precomputed once (k = 1..5 cells along the line to the origin).
8. **Wall line on the building edge (Lab).** `GET /api/runs/{id}/worldmap` adds `faceClassIds` and `surfaceClassIds`. `js/worldmap.js`
   draws the wall line (yellow, same colour / width) on every cell border between a face-class cell and a 4-neighbour surface cell of the
   snapshot shown (one path). The fitted `faceEdgeLines` are no longer drawn (they stay in hazards.json; the rule still uses them).
   Face (magenta) and edge (cyan) cells stay.
9. **Coordinates on unmapped cells.** `cellText()` for an unmapped cell: the same first line as a mapped cell (clock · steps · m ahead ·
   m left/right · cell col / row), then `unmapped`.
