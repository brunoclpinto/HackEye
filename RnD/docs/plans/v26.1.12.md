## Decisions
1. **Reach:** movers are spoken within **10 steps** (`movingDangerSteps` 15 → 10), the same as static dangers.
2. **Beeps:** only on a collision course and at **≤ 5 steps**. `tones.bySteps` starts at 5 (e.g. [[5,0.5],[3,0.25],[2,0.12]]). Beyond that, speech carries the warning.
3. **Re-announce on a motion change:** a mover already announced is announced again, once per change, when its state becomes `approaching` on a collision course ("person approaching at 12:15"). This is the only re-announcement; dedupe per track and state.
4. **Motion-state accuracy:**
   - **Thresholds:** "standing" needs measured |own longitudinal speed| < 0.3 m/s held for ≥ 1 s **and** no lateral motion. Approaching and receding use 0.4 m/s with hysteresis.
   - **Uncertain cases:** in between, or when the measurement is short or noisy, the plain phrase `"{label} at {clock} in {steps}"` is said. A wrong state is worse than none.
   - **Lateral motion (investigation):** the existing lateral-offset derivative is noise-dominated (pole p90 1.4–2.4 m/s). Try the image-space bearing rate of the track (bbox centre or contact x) minus the camera yaw rate from `motion.npz` dx (the heading series), smoothed over ~1 s. Validate it on static poles (≈ 0) and on track 743 at 40–42 s (moving left).
   - **New state:** if the rate separates them with margin, add `crossing`: template `"{label} moving left at {clock}"` / `"… moving right …"`, no distance. Otherwise leave such tracks as the plain phrase. Report the numbers either way.
5. **Unknown motion near the user:** find why track 2147 has no closing at 90.8 s (no depth history? too short?), and make motion available earlier if possible (e.g. ≥ 0.5 s / 3 points from depth). If motion is still unknown, a person within 5 steps and |lateral| ≤ `movingLaneHalfWidthM` beeps (conservative: unknown is treated as possibly approaching for beeps only).
6. **"Curb" pronunciation:** the speech text gets a pronunciation map in `audio.js` (display text unchanged): `curb` → `kerb`. It is Lab-only and logged in client-log entries as `spoken`. The user verifies by ear.
