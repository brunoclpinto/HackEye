# World map vs learned decision model — evaluation and recommendation

## Context

The user wants to discuss the next evolution of HackEye's collision logic, as a stepping stone to
**navigation assistance**: classifying paths as safe, safe-ish or unsafe for a visually impaired walker.
Two candidates:

- **A. World map:** build a map from the frames, starting as a flat 2D plane where items lie flat and
  evolving to 3D, where items have volume.
- **B. Learned model:** an open-source model that runs fully on a mobile device, similar to V-JEPA
  ("jev"). From the frames and masks it would infer collisions, dangers and decisions, ideally faster
  and more accurately than the rules.

**Where we are (v26.1.16):**
- **Per frame:** semantic segmentation (Yolo26m-sem, 736p), DA2 metric depth with a ground-plane fit
  plus body calibration, and ego motion (cadence, residual optical flow, heading).
- **Over time:** image-space items with one contact point each, tracks, and `_WorldMemory`, which
  dead-reckons announced items into a 2D world frame for object permanence (`src/collision.py`). The
  collision pass costs ~2.9 ms per frame.
- **Already agreed (DECISIONS v26.1.10):** no end-to-end learned model until review labels exist.
  V-JEPA 2 is not viable on an iPhone 12 and is only a Lab candidate.
- **Recent bugs:** the 10.9 s building that sticks out into the path and the walls running along the
  pavement are both failures of the "one contact point in image space" representation, not of the rules
  themselves.

## A. World map (2D → 2.5D → 3D)

**Correction to the premise:** a global, stitched panorama-style map is expensive and fragile. What
navigation needs is a **local, ego-centric bird's-eye grid** (e.g. 20 × 20 m around the user, 10 cm
cells), fused over the last ~10–20 s and moved by the ego pose each frame. That is cheap:
- **Per frame:** project the existing 240×135 class grid and the depth map onto the ground plane we
  already fit. That's ~32k cells, under 1 ms in numpy, trivial on an iPhone.
- **Over time:** decay and fuse the cells. We already have the inputs: depth, plane, camera height
  from body calibration, and ego speed and heading.

**Pros**
- **Fixes the representation errors at the source.** Walls along the path, a building sticking out,
  curbs and poles all become occupied or surface cells. The rule "is anything solid in my corridor
  within N steps" becomes a lookup in the map, with no per-class contact-point heuristics.
- **The safe / safe-ish / unsafe path falls out naturally.** It's a traversability layer per cell
  computed from things we already compute:
  - Surface class: sidewalk or pedestrian area is safe; crosswalk and bike lane are safe-ish; road is
    unsafe.
  - Occupancy and the clearance width of free space.
  - Curb edges.
  - Movers' predicted cells.

  Path guidance ("sidewalk continues at 11, narrowing to 1 m in 8 steps") then becomes a search over
  the grid.
- **Object permanence and less re-announcing for free.** It generalises `_WorldMemory`, which today
  stores only announced items.
- **Explainable and reviewable.** A top-down Lab panel shows exactly why something was said, so the
  user's review loop keeps working.
- **Scales cleanly to the 2.5D / 3D stage.** A height per cell from depth catches **overhangs at head
  height** (awnings, branches, open truck doors). A white cane misses these, so they are a high-value
  safety class. Full 3D volumes are an extension, not a rewrite.
- **Doesn't depend on any particular camera.** It works with GoPro, iPhone or Meta glasses, since the
  only inputs are seg + depth + motion.
- **It is the data engine for B.** Map plus review labels make the training set a learned model needs.

**Cons / risks**
- **Pose drift.** The map is only as good as ego motion. Cadence × step speed is noisy, the walking
  speed estimate already reads 1.4–1.8× fast, and turning in place is misread. Mitigation: a short
  horizon (local map, 10–20 s), plus a cheap visual-odometry term from the optical flow we already
  compute. On the iPhone the IMU (and ARKit VIO when the phone itself is the camera) gives far better
  pose.
- **Monocular depth errors.** Scale and edge bleeding put obstacles in the wrong cells. Fusing over
  frames helps, and the plane-fit calibration is already there.
- **Dynamic objects need their own layer.** People and vehicles stay as tracks with predicted cells
  and are not burned into the map.
- **Head mount:** the head turns independently of walking direction, so the body heading must come
  from the IMU (the `_walking_axis_offset_m` hook already exists for this).
- **Engineering effort:** moderate. New module, a Lab top-down view, and porting the rules to read
  the map.

## B. Learned on-device decision model (V-JEPA-like)

**Pros**
- **Handles fuzzy, long-tail scenes** the rules don't encode (cluttered markets, construction, odd
  shapes) and learns timing and saliency from examples.
- **One model can output many decisions** (danger, path class, what to say) once trained.

**Cons**
- **No data.** About 8 reviews, a few hundred labels on 2–3 recordings. A collision and navigation
  model needs thousands of labelled minutes across places, light and weather. That is the dominant
  blocker, not compute.
- **Not "faster".** The rules cost ~3 ms per frame, so any model adds latency. V-JEPA 2 (300 M–1 B
  parameters) does not fit the iPhone budget, which is already ~2–6 fps with the seg and depth models.
  A mobile-sized video model means distilling or training our own small temporal head anyway.
- **Safety and validation.** Silent misses from a black box are the worst failure for this user group.
  Proving it is safer than the rules needs a large held-out test set, which we don't have. The user's
  review loop ("this should not be said", "say X here") cannot be applied as a direct fix; it becomes
  retraining.
- **Licensing:** many video foundation models are CC-BY-NC or carry Meta terms. That's acceptable for
  this non-commercial app, but it still has to be tracked in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`.

## Recommendation

**Choose A (local ego-centric map) now. Treat B as a later layer on top of A, not as an alternative.**
- A is the better stepping stone into navigation, because "safe, safe-ish or unsafe path" is a spatial
  question about the free space ahead, and the map is exactly that representation.
- When B comes, it should take the **map (bird's-eye tensors) plus mask history as input**, trained on
  labels A produces automatically plus the user's reviews. That's a far smaller, mobile-friendly and
  data-efficient model than one reasoning from raw frames.

## Proposed roadmap (milestone v26.2 = navigation map, per the version scheme)

1. **Feasibility spike (Lab only, no rule changes).**
   - **Build:** `src/worldmap.py`, a 2D ego-centric grid fused from class grid + depth + ego pose, with
     a top-down panel in the Lab.
   - **Measure on Chest-0004 0–155 s and Chest-0002:**
     - Cost in ms per frame.
     - Map consistency: does a wall stay a straight line over 10 s, and does the pole re-observed after
       passing land in the same cell?
     - Drift.
   - **Decision gate:** is the pose good enough, or is visual odometry needed first?
2. **Rules read the map.**
   - **What changes:** lane occupancy, along-path walls and protrusions come from the bird's-eye grid
     instead of image contact points.
   - **Pass criterion:** all review fixtures, no ✓ lost.
3. **Traversability layer.** Mark cells as safe, safe-ish or unsafe and compute the free-corridor
   width, first as a silent Lab overlay. The user then reviews it like speech.
4. **2.5D.** Add a height per cell to detect overhangs and obstacles at head height.
5. **Learned layer (B).** Only once 3–4 have produced a labelled corpus.

**Workers:** Sonnet workers for the build (1 for `worldmap.py` + metrics, 1 for the Lab top-down
panel), Opus for planning and review, as usual.
